# MPCut release notes

## Unreleased

- **Check which projects a build opens.** The current reader and writer use schema 9 for sequence, multicam and still projects. Open older projects in a compatible build. `mpcut --version --json` and the completed `cli_version_read` job report the build ID, supported schemas and detector capabilities. [Project format](docs/CUT-FORMAT.md)
- **Resume an unattended edit.** A files-only MCP job keeps its operation receipt for seven days. After a disconnect, use the original UUID to recover the result. A worker interrupted after starting an edit can report `outcome_unknown`. Inspect the file before starting another edit. [File jobs and recovery](docs/HEADLESS.md)
- **Review the picture and approve the export.** Compare changes to mask geometry and tile assignments in retained history, fetch a saved program frame as an inline JPEG, then pass the dry run’s `plan_token` to render the reviewed project. A change to the project, source identities, settings, destination or build invalidates the approval. [CLI guide](docs/CLI.md#render-one-output)
- **Finish a saved edit without opening a window.** Stage or discard pending Tiles choices, apply a reviewed batch of camera switches, set the project’s output settings, protect a detected multicam pause, and name a recurring scene. Recognition jobs can be found by project and reconciled after a timing change. Media searches can include files needed by Undo. [Headless workflows](docs/HEADLESS.md) · [Transcript editing](docs/TRANSCRIPTS.md)
- **Control the live editor.** Agents can close the history sheet before saving, start a still-only timeline in a blank window, switch the editor viewer between Source and Output, and set panel docking and transcript follow preferences. Reverse shuttle now finishes at the beginning of the timeline even when the audio output misses its final playback callback. [Live editor workflows](docs/MCP-AGENT-GUIDE.md)
- **Settings in eight pages.** ⌘, opens a window with General, Editing, Playback & Monitors, Media & Analysis, Transcripts, Export, Storage and Automation pages. Search above the sidebar finds a setting by name or keyword and opens its page. The window can be resized, reopens on the last page and works with no project open. A typed number is saved on Return, Tab, a click elsewhere or a page change, and a short notice under the field says when an entry was limited to its range, rounded or not a number. **Restore Defaults…** lists what will change, when it takes effect and what it keeps. Force SDR for the viewer and the Video Monitor, clean feed, the panel position and the multicam track label width are now there too, and a change reaches every open window. [Settings](docs/USAGE.md#set-editing-and-export-defaults)
- **Start new work with your usual choices.** Settings now gives new work its starting values: the re-encode codec, quality, ProRes quality and container, the multicam audio layout, Save Export Result JSON, opening the timeline in Resolve, still, gap and Space Clips durations and the gap color, the new stills timeline format, the speed, cut sections and transcript follow state of new windows, the Find Tiles layout, grid and scan detail and the dialogue switch options, the transcription engine and job options, and the transcript edit boundary, tightening pause, filler terms and Export Transcript format. A choice changed in a sheet applies to that window or job and leaves Settings unchanged. A new stills timeline at 23.976, 29.97, 59.94 or 119.88 fps uses the exact NTSC rate, such as 24000/1001.
- **Choose how long to play around a cut.** Preview Join and pause previews in both editors play the seconds set in **Settings → Playback & Monitors**. Multicam pause review now plays 1 second on each side of the cut, as the other previews do, instead of 0.75 seconds.
- **Know when an export finishes.** **Settings → Export** can show a finished export in Finder, post a notification while MPCut is in the background, or play a sound, and can start export panels in the project folder, the source folder or a folder you choose. MPCut keeps the Mac awake during exports and transcription. After a multicam export, **Show in Finder** and **File → Show Last Export in Finder** reveal the file, and the multicam save panel suggests the project’s name.
- **Fewer questions.** Choose what opening several recordings from Finder does, or select **Don’t ask again** on that alert, the scene rescan alert or the export tier alert. **Reset All Warnings** in **Settings → General** brings them back. The “Exported …” message after a project export without warnings can be turned off.
- **Automatic scans.** **Settings → Media & Analysis** can scan each recording you add for scenes, pauses or both, and only those recordings. Both start off.
- **See and limit what MPCut keeps.** **Settings → Storage** lists the space used by generated stills, transcription results, job scratch, drafts, suggested cameras and agent receipts, with each folder’s location, Show in Finder and Clear where clearing is safe. Clear says what it reclaimed or that nothing was removed, what it left in use, and any files it did not recognize as MPCut’s. You can limit the shared cache folder, in gigabytes to three decimals, and move untitled drafts to the Trash after a number of days. **Settings → General** can offer to recover untitled projects at launch and sets how many items **File → Open Recent** lists.
- **Transcripts settings.** Choose the Transcriberrr helper, check its status and engines, set the reader text size, and set the line length, line count, cue length and speaker labels of captions exported from the app. The transcription **Language** field offers Automatic or English, the language the engine accepts.
- **Video Monitor position and frame display.** The Video Monitor can reopen where you last placed it. Set **Settings → Editing → Time display** to **Frames** to read the playhead, duration, marks and fine ruler labels as non-drop `MM:SS:FF` at the timeline’s frame rate, in both editors and in open windows at once. Timelines without one constant frame rate keep milliseconds, and so do source and transcript times, reports, files and agent results. [Time display](docs/USAGE.md#time-display)
- **Agent permissions.** **Settings → Automation** shows the CLI and MCP helper paths with **Copy**, copies an agent configuration for this copy of MPCut, and has **Let agents change settings** and **Let agents replace existing project files**. Both start on. With one off, the refused call returns `settings_write_disabled` or `file_replace_disabled`. `preferences_read` lists every setting, `workspace_panel_show` can open Settings on a named page, and a live export or transcription that omits the codec, quality, multicam audio layout or engine uses the editor’s Settings values. File jobs and the CLI keep factory values. [Agent guide](docs/MCP-AGENT-GUIDE.md)
- **Check for Updates.** **Help → Check for Updates…** opens the MPCut releases page in your browser. MPCut itself makes no network request.
- **Every fallback says so.** When an export has to settle for less than it was asked, it now tells you instead of carrying on quietly. A participant or ISO render that finds no hardware encoder session for constant quality, as when many ISOs render at once, still finishes at the tier's average bit rate and says so, and an ISO movie reused from an earlier run keeps that note in its manifest. A Re-encode whose hardware decoder fails partway decodes in software and says that too. These notes come from writing, so a dry run cannot show them: CLI and MCP results list them as `written_warnings`, beside the `warnings` a dry run and its export share, and the export sheet, the Export Queue and the saved Export Result JSON show them. A participant composition says when H.264 shortens a recording deeper than 8 bits, when an irregular recording rate leaves the canvas at 30 fps, and when a recording larger than 8192 pixels scales the canvas down. The editor names HEVC when an HDR composition cannot be written in H.264, and Audio Only says when it mixes program lanes to stereo. Resolve timeline exports from the app and MCP list the cuts they round and anything else they could not keep, as the CLI already did. A copy into MOV or MP4 names the movie metadata the container has no field for, such as a camera's `com.apple.quicktime` keys, which colour description it keeps when the source has both an ICC profile and colour tags, and a cube-map or tiled 360° projection that MOV cannot describe; a copy into MP4 now keeps the 360° projection boxes it used to drop. A movie re-encode, a multicam program and a still program name the source metadata they do not carry, such as a title, creation date or camera model. A copy into MOV or MP4 also says when the source timecode breaks inside a kept range (the copied track counts on over it), when it has a 24-hour wrap or negative-time flag MOV cannot state, when it counts per field pair, and when a Matroska or MXF timecode tag has no track to go into; when HDR10+ or SMPTE 2094 dynamic metadata, LCEVC data or other Matroska block additions are left behind; and when a MOV colour box cannot hold a full-range flag the picture does not state itself. An AC-3 or E-AC-3 copy whose final frame cannot be read still ends at its last audible sample, measured by the packet's own length. A copy stops instead of carrying on when the muxer refuses its movie clock, edit-list, track-ID or colour settings. A lossless camera soundtrack in a still project written to MP4 or M4A takes ALAC or FLAC before AAC, and says so if it still ends up as AAC. Chapters without a readable start, PNG and TIFF frames written without the source's alpha channel, an FCPXML import that rebuilds a multicam group whose saved MPCut editing no longer matches, a transcript edit planned on a recording that could not be indexed, and an interchange timeline given a picture format because no clip has picture are all reported. `silence --output` now reports `encoded_edge_results`, a multicam silence export lists its `warnings`, MCP export plans and renders name the conversion `audio: auto` applied, an MCP sound-only encode without an audio action follows Auto as the CLI does instead of always converting to PCM, the multicam export review and completion message list a still program's warnings, a Resolve timeline of a sequence with replacement audio says what it omits, a multicam JPEG comparison counts the camera frames it could not write, and `--replace-audio` with an `--order` that leaves out every clip of its source is refused instead of being ignored. The bundled FFmpeg build stops if a required encoder is missing.

- **Copied sound at a join says where other players put it.** Lossless Copy and Smart Trim join AAC ranges in MOV, MP4 and M4A with one edit-list entry each, and Apple's players play every join to the sample, as the source decodes. Players that apply an interior entry only at whole AAC frames, such as FFmpeg-based ones, play on past the cut and leave out the start of the next range, so the plan and the export now name each such join with both distances in milliseconds, and how far past the end such a player can run. An AC-3 or E-AC-3 copy joined between frames, or started inside its source, now says that up to 5.3 ms after each join is decoded with another frame's overlap and can click. The notes appear in a dry run and its export alike, in CLI and MCP `warnings`, the export sheet, the Export Queue and the audio lanes of multicam programs. Converting the audio writes one continuous track that avoids both.

- **AAC in MKV: exact trims in every copy, exact joins with Smart Trim.** Matroska has no edit list, so a copy cannot place an AAC join inside a frame or give the frames after it their history, and Lossless Copy refuses such a join; it now says Smart Trim can make it. Smart Trim encodes the few AAC frames around each join, about 40 to 85 ms, at the source's rate, channels and bit rate, and trims one of them with Matroska DiscardPadding, so FFmpeg-based players decode the join to the sample and the copied frames on either side unchanged. Before it writes, Smart Trim decodes each join as a player would and moves it by a frame, or refuses with the reason, if the encoded frames would not meet the copied ones within the codec's own error. It picks FFmpeg's or Apple's AAC encoder to match the copied frames' window shape. The plan, the export, the export sheet, the Export Queue and CLI and MCP JSON list these frames under `encoded_audio_edges`, with `encoded_audio_duration` and the `copy_and_encode` audio action, and the new tier factor `join_aac_frames` says Smart Trim makes such a join. HE-AAC, AAC with a program configuration element, and ranges too short for the frames are refused with the reason. Lossless Copy and Smart Trim also start and end an MKV movie inside an AAC frame losslessly, as a MOV edit list does: the copied frames stay byte-identical, the first comes with the frame before it, which the track's codec delay hides along with the samples before the cut, and the last discards the samples after the cut, so a single trimmed range now copies into MKV and the tier badge and the export sheet agree on it. A sound-only export has no Smart Trim, so it still refuses these joins and points to converting the audio. Two older Matroska faults are fixed with it: an MKV copy of an AAC track from MKV or MOV now always states the track's frame duration, so MPCut can open and copy its own output again, and a Matroska AAC track with a codec delay now starts on the sample FFmpeg decodes first, not 16 samples (a third of a millisecond at 48 kHz) later, so its planned length matches the decoded one. Indexes of such tracks are rebuilt.

- **The fallback preview follows a change of sample description, and says when it skips pictures or changes decoder.** An MP4 or MOV track can switch to a new H.264 or HEVC sample description at a keyframe, as joins of differently encoded clips do, with the new parameter sets only in the description. The FFmpeg preview kept decoding with the first description's sets, so after an H.264 change, or an HEVC change of picture parameter sets, every picture came out wrong or was rejected and skipped to the next keyframe without a word. It now returns the pictures it holds, reopens its decoder at that keyframe, and matches AVFoundation picture for picture in playback, seeks, scrubbing and reverse play; the Smart Trim edge decoder, which such sources do not yet reach, does the same. A description that only lists the same sets again changes nothing, and at an HEVC open keyframe the new sets go to the running decoder, so the leading pictures that predict from the pictures before it still decode. A seek into such leading pictures in MP4 or MOV no longer starts at the open keyframe after them, which used to drop the picture asked for. Where the preview still skips pictures, after a damaged picture, a description that changes at a picture that is not a keyframe, or leading pictures it cannot reconstruct (named with their times), a notice beside the picture says so for that pass and a still at such a position names the reason, and damaged audio that plays as silence is named under the picture too. When FFmpeg decodes a viewer or multicam picture because AVFoundation could not, the viewer's status (the badge's tooltip and menu) says so and why, and `project_editor_state_read` reports a sequence's `preview`: the decoder in use, why the FFmpeg preview took over, and the notices the viewer shows.

- **Smart Trim keeps more signalling or refuses.** An edge picture decoded with frame packing, display orientation, an HEVC or H.264 time code, alternative transfer characteristics, or ATSC active format description or bar data in its SEI is no longer re-encoded without them: the export, and except for an H.264 picture timing time code the plan too, refuses it and asks for a verified boundary, as it does for captions and colour metadata.

- **Tiles found whole on Zoom calls.** With TileTrail meeting detector revision 7, a gallery and the cameras beside a share are placed from the shape of the layout, so a dark room, a shelf, a dark desktop wallpaper or an avatar card no longer cuts a tile into pieces, and a warmly lit room no longer flickers to camera off. A 16:9 call window recorded inside a wider frame stays one tile, where its own gray side bars used to read as two camera-off tiles. On 46 two-hour recordings adaptive scans went from 25,074 feed tracks to 298, and every tile was right on all 588 sampled frames, up from 494, and on all 66 frames sampled from 11 recordings that no tuning looked at, up from 44, and all 72 frames from 6 more. A recording whose edit ends on its last stored sample now scans to completion with that sample's width as unknown coverage, where it used to stop as incomplete and could not be accepted. [Tiles editing guide](docs/PARTICIPANTS.md)

- **One decision per tile in Needs a Look.** A tile that waits for a feed in many places now shows as one row, and **Same Feed** or **Different Feed** settles all of them. **Same Feed** folds the tile's own feed into the earlier one so the person keeps one feed, and **Different Feed** keeps the scan's own feed for the tile instead of creating a new one. Issues carry `group_id` and `group_count` in `tiles inspect` and `tiles export-isos`, and a `tiles link` without a range, after `tiles feeds merge` for the same person, settles a group. A feed shown in another tile of the same kind at that moment is no longer offered as a possible match. On 46 recordings, 3,416 review items became 142 decisions. [Tiles editing guide](docs/PARTICIPANTS.md)

- **More copied, less re-encoded.** H.264 and HEVC in MOV, MP4 and MKV can end a copied section between keyframes, including constant-rate footage with B-frames, so Lossless Copy accepts more cuts and Smart Trim re-encodes less at the end of a clip. A copied section still starts on a keyframe. With **Snap To → Keyframes**, an end handle on such footage lands on these points too. Cached indexes of H.264, HEVC and MOV-family files are rebuilt once. [Export modes](docs/EXPORT-MODES.md)

- **Copies keep more, and say what they leave out.** Chapters, the timecode track of a picture-only copy, and the cover art and Matroska attachments of a recording without picture now travel with Lossless Copy and Smart Trim. A re-encode keeps chapters, a sound-only conversion keeps cover art and carries chapters onto its kept ranges in M4A, MP4, MOV, MKA and MP3 (FLAC, WAV, CAF and Opus files omit them and say so), and a movie with a QuickTime chapter track can be copied again. Anything the chosen container cannot hold appears under the summary in the export sheet and queue and as `warnings` in CLI and MCP results. The Export Queue and the tier details also show how much video Smart Trim encodes.

- **Lossless sound stays lossless, and Auto keeps the source format.** When Re-encode has to convert a track, Auto writes it in the source's own codec and settings where the container and the bundled FFmpeg allow: PCM at its sample format, FLAC and ALAC at their word length, and AAC, MP3 and Opus at the source's rate. Where the container cannot carry the codec, or no encoder exists (AC-3, Vorbis, TrueHD), a lossless track falls back to PCM and a lossy one to AAC, and the track's reason says why. Mixed timelines, multicam lanes of one format and still soundtracks follow the same rule. A converted AAC track is never written below the bit rate of its source. Sound decoded from a lossy or floating-point source is written as floating-point PCM, so peaks above full scale are no longer clipped, and reports state each track’s codec, bit rate and bit depth. Still projects accept FLAC and ALAC soundtracks from M4A, MOV and MKA and copy AAC, AC-3 and E-AC-3 soundtracks. Copies the muxer would refuse while writing — FLAC, Opus or TrueHD into MOV, PCM without a stated channel layout into MP4, and for still soundtracks Opus or ALAC into Matroska and MP2 into MP4 — are refused when planned with the containers that carry them, so a dry run and the export agree. An MP3 or MP2 recording in MKA can be attached whole as a still soundtrack.

- **Lossless frames and truer colour.** `review frames export --format png` or `tiff` writes frames that keep the source’s bit depth and colour profile, including PQ and HLG signal. JPEG stays the default. Exported HLG frames are tone-mapped like the SDR preview, software-decoded limited-range frames keep full black and white levels, untagged HD frames are read as BT.709 as the native preview shows them, and a gain-map photo on an SDR timeline uses its own SDR rendition.

- **Copies of movies with subtitles, data and attachments.** A movie with subtitle tracks (mov_text, SRT, ASS, WebVTT), GoPro data, cover art or Matroska attachments is copied instead of refused, with cues that cross a cut shortened to the kept range. A multicam copy carries the reference camera's chapters onto the program clock. Whatever a container cannot hold is listed in the export's warnings.

- **H.264 from MKV copies into MP4 and MOV.** H.264 in Matroska without B-frames was refused for MP4 and MOV copies as missing decode times; it now copies, and Matroska H.264 or HEVC with uneven picture spacing and B-frames ends between keyframes too. Copies that ended after a held picture used to come out a little short; they now run to the planned end.

- **Copies that start after a parameter set change decode cleanly.** A Lossless Copy or Smart Trim of H.264 or HEVC that starts after the stream changed its parameter sets used to describe the range with the old ones, so the copy decoded with errors. Each range, and each Smart Trim edge, now starts from the sets the source holds there. HEVC whose sets change inside a picture group is copied as `hvc1` where a new ID allows it, and otherwise the plan says the copy uses `hev1`, which AVFoundation and QuickTime cannot play.

- **Spatial video and HEVC with alpha copy and Smart Trim whole.** A Lossless Copy of HEVC with alpha, as Apple's apps and VideoToolbox write it, used to come out without the parameter sets of its alpha layer, so AVFoundation and QuickTime could not open the copy at all; the sets now travel in an `lhvC` box, and the copy plays with its alpha and its premultiplied or straight alpha mode. An MV-HEVC spatial video from iPhone or Apple Vision Pro copied into MP4 now keeps which view is which eye, the camera baseline, the disparity adjustment and the field of view, as MOV already did, and where its second view starts a picture group beside a base picture with no leading pictures, as in Apple's own encodes without B-frames, it can be cut at that keyframe, where only its first picture was accepted before. MKV has no place for the parameter sets of a second view or an alpha layer, so a copy into it is refused and names the layer instead of writing a file that shows one eye or no alpha. Smart Trim now cuts both between keyframes: AVFoundation decodes every layer of an edge and VideoToolbox encodes it again with its alpha, in the source's premultiplied or straight mode, or with both views under the source's layer IDs and eyes, where Smart Trim used to accept only keyframe cuts. H.264 with auxiliary alpha pictures, MVC or SVC now counts as having more than one layer too; Smart Trim cannot encode those layers, so it cuts such pictures (and HEVC Main 10 or depth layers) at keyframes and the error or warning says so, where H.264 edges used to lose their alpha without a word. Re-encode, whose FFmpeg encoder keeps one picture (and marks any alpha premultiplied), refuses every picture with more than one layer and names what it would drop. The parameter-set tracking behind copies that start mid-stream now follows the sets of every HEVC layer and H.264's extension and subset sets, and where a stream's sets still cannot be followed, where the samples of HEVC could not be read to plan its `hvc1` description, or where a Matroska copy of reordered H.264 cannot declare its reorder depth, the export's warnings say so; a copy that would be left `hev1` after being planned as `hvc1` now fails instead. Cached indexes of H.264, and of HEVC with more than one layer, are rebuilt once. [Export modes](docs/EXPORT-MODES.md)

- **Auto keeps lossless sound lossless across joins.** A sound-only re-encode with `--audio auto` writes ALAC from ALAC and FLAC from FLAC, and a lossless source whose own codec the named container cannot hold takes the nearest lossless one (FLAC into .m4a becomes ALAC). The Export Queue and MCP queue renders report what each Smart Trim edge wrote.

- **SRT into MP4, and MP4 captions into MKV.** A movie re-encode converts a text subtitle track that the chosen container cannot hold as is, instead of leaving it out: SRT, ASS and WebVTT become 3GPP text (`mov_text`) in MP4 and MOV, and 3GPP text becomes SRT in MKV. Cues are trimmed and moved with the kept ranges exactly as a copied track's are, then converted, so each keeps its text, start and length. 3GPP text has no positions, alignment, outlines or animation, so ASS styling beyond bold, italic, underline, colour, font and size is dropped; the warnings name what each converted track loses. The bundled FFmpeg helper gains its text subtitle encoders for this (about 16 KB); a helper built before them keeps the old warning. Bitmap subtitles are still left out and named. [Export modes](docs/EXPORT-MODES.md)

- **Overlapping subtitles keep every line in MP4 and MOV, with their colours, sizes and fonts.** 3GPP text shows one cue at a time, so when Re-encode converted SRT, ASS or WebVTT with overlapping cues, a cue ended as soon as the next began and of cues that started together only the last was shown, including cues that all crossed the start of a kept range. The track is now split at every cue start and end, and each part shows the text of every cue on screen then, one under the other in cue order, each line in its own style. Every conversion to 3GPP text also keeps styling that FFmpeg's encoder used to drop or garble: SRT font colours and ASS `\c` colours, ASS `\alpha`, the size after a closing SRT font size (it was written as size zero), SRT font faces and ASS fonts that no style uses, and the colour and size of a non-default ASS style around an override such as `\i1` (they fell back to the default style). Font sizes are scaled from the script's height to the picture's, where they used to stay in script units (an SRT's 16 was 16 pixels on a 1080-line picture, now 60), and a size above 255 pixels is written at 255 instead of wrapping around to a small one. Identical ASS lines drawn on two layers, such as an outline under its text, show once in the top layer's style instead of twice. The warnings say what is still lost: strikethrough, positions and alignment, ASS outlines, shadows, scaling, spacing, rotation, blur, animation and karaoke, flattened layers, sizes above 255 pixels, fonts whose name has a comma, and a highlight that two ASS cues shown together both set, which runs from the first to the last. Lossless Copy and Smart Trim never convert subtitles, and 3GPP text they copy cannot overlap. [Export modes](docs/EXPORT-MODES.md)

- **Re-encode keeps subtitles, and Opus keeps its rate.** A movie re-encode now carries subtitle tracks (mov_text, SRT, ASS, WebVTT) and GoPro data as packet copies on the output timeline, with cues outside the kept ranges dropped and cues that cross a cut shortened, into the containers that hold them; a track that would need converting is named in the warnings. A converted Opus track is encoded at its source's own average rate to the nearest 1 kbps instead of the nearest customary step, which missed by up to a tenth (141 kbps was written at 128 kbps). With `--audio auto`, clip spacing that needs generated silence converts compressed sound in its own codec and says so, instead of asking for a conversion. A quality chosen for a still-only project now applies, while an untouched setting keeps the still default.

- **More end points, and edges that match the source.** VP8, VP9 and AV1 copies can end between keyframes wherever no hidden picture from past the cut would be kept, and MOV and MP4 footage with B-frames and varying frame durations ends inside a picture group too. Smart Trim gives an edge on still or slow footage at least the bytes of the source's own keyframe, an AV1 edge is retried more gently, and the export reports what each edge wrote. A dry run now refuses the in-band parameter sets and embedded HDR, caption or Dolby metadata that used to stop the export itself.

- **Copies that open everywhere.** HEVC copied from Matroska into MP4 or MOV is labelled so AVFoundation plays it, VP9 copied into MP4 carries its codec description, and Opus copied to `.opus` or written into MP4 decodes to exactly its source's samples.

- **Preview and frames agree.** The fallback preview, the native preview and frame export read an untagged picture the same way, including its matrix, primaries and transfer. Frames of stored JPEG video are written byte for byte when nothing needs changing. A still-only project keeps deep stills in HEVC Main 10 and follows the chosen ProRes profile and the Smaller File quality.

- **Joins and copies that every player reads.** A Matroska join of VideoToolbox H.264 declares its reorder depth, so no picture is lost at the join; HEVC whose parameter sets change at a keyframe gets one `hvc1` description per set, so AVFoundation plays it; and AC-3 or E-AC-3 copied into MOV or MP4 ends at its last audible sample.

- **Dry runs and film grain.** A still project with camera footage now finds, before writing, the captions or colour metadata that would stop Smart Trim. An edge re-encoded from footage that carries film grain parameters is written, and the plan says those pictures no longer carry them.

- **Participant renders keep their depth, colour and timing.** Deep recordings stay deep through compositing, SDR output is BT.709 on every macOS version, H.264 and HEVC use Re-encode's quality tiers, scaling uses Lanczos in both directions, and the canvas takes the recording's frame rate unless you choose one.

- **Colour that matches the preview in more cases.** Frames from pictures tagged with only a transfer, only primaries or no matrix, and the SDR preview of BT.2020 HDR without a matrix, now show what the native preview shows. Still projects record rendition policy 2.

- **What the app shows.** The export sheet gives each converted track's bit rate or word length and the reason for a fallback, multicam copies list what they leave out, and a Smart Trim export reports what each encoded edge wrote.

- **Gapless MP3 copies.** MP3 copies write their gapless header fields themselves, so a cropped MP3 keeps its exact length when it is copied again with either FFmpeg 9.0 or 9.1.

- **Leaner analysis sidecars.** Frame indexes, waveforms and silence results are stored in packed forms. A sidecar left by an early build is replaced the next time analysis is saved and can be removed with Clear Caches, older files are repacked in the background after they are read, and silence results and timeline thumbnails are kept within a fixed budget. [Desktop guide](docs/USAGE.md#save-and-reuse-analysis)

- **Lighter transcription leftovers and transcript memory.** A finished job keeps each track’s result and levels packed, recognition shared with Transcriberrr is stored about twenty times smaller once both apps are updated, and an open transcript builds only the view being read. [Transcript guide](docs/TRANSCRIPTS.md)

- **Shorter IDs.** New clips, stills, sources and transcript records get a 12-character ID in place of a 36-character one. Existing IDs are unchanged. [Project format](docs/CUT-FORMAT.md)

- **Fast participant scans.** A scan now reads a recording’s keyframes first and decodes the frames between two of them only where the later keyframe shows a change. A two-hour 1080p Zoom recording whose layout changed twice scanned in under 2 seconds, where analyzing every frame took about 10 minutes, and reported both changes on the same frames. A 2.5-hour recording with frequent changes took about 4 seconds. Stretches skipped this way are marked as inferred coverage; choose `--sampling every-frame` to verify a redaction. Every-frame, sparse and combined scene scans share the same reader and take about a minute for two hours: small H.264 pictures decode on several threads, pictures convert side by side, and the recording is hashed while it is scanned. A scan also holds far less memory. With TileTrail 0.4.1, a meeting scan keeps an established tile through the few frames where a dark band or a flat moment makes the detector cut it in two or merge it with its neighbours, which removed most false leave and enter events on real recordings; saved meeting scans are rebuilt. `mpcut participants scan --output SCAN.json --summary` prints the scan’s state, counts and coverage in place of a second copy of the report. [Participant guide](docs/PARTICIPANTS.md)

- **Still images with lossless sound.** Attach a recording to a still sequence, fit the picture to its exact duration, and choose **One frame per still** to store each image once for its full hold. ## 1.0.0

First public release for Apple Silicon Macs running macOS 14 or later.

- **Edit on one timeline.** Add and reorder compatible recordings, trim clip edges, split at the playhead, and delete a marked range. The timeline includes frame stepping, keyframe navigation, and editable time.
- **Export without re-encoding.** Copy retained video and audio samples into a new file. MPCut checks clip compatibility, cut boundaries, and the output container. Standard ProRes supports cuts on every picture. H.264 and HEVC require verified splice points.
- **Preview on the Mac.** The app uses hardware decoding when the Mac supports the video stream. Eligible exports can also run headlessly.
- **Automate edits.** The bundled `mpcut` CLI inspects files, accepts ordered clips and exact timestamps, reads JSON on stdin, and supports dry runs. Use `mpcut --version` to identify the installed release.

### Installation

Download `MPCut-1.0.0-macOS-arm64.zip`, expand it, and move MPCut.app into Applications. To verify the download, place `SHA256SUMS.txt` beside the ZIP and run `shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt` in that directory. The app is ad-hoc signed and has no Apple notarization ticket.

### Compatibility

Joining requires matching streams. RAW video and standalone WAV files are excluded. PCM embedded in supported movies is preserved. WebM supports whole-file copies and verified cuts, with restrictions on discontinuous Opus and Vorbis audio. Runtime qualification used an M4 Max on macOS 27.0.1.
